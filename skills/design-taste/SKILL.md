# Design taste

Taste is a set of habits you can check, not a mood. Use this skill alongside pattern-aesthetics-craft each time you design, and before showing any work.

## Habits
1. **Reduce until it breaks, then add one thing back.** Every part must earn its place. Ask what the work loses if a part is removed. If nothing, remove it.
2. **Construction first, ornament second.** Ornament grows from the structure (Owen Jones). Nothing floats, and every curve comes from a parent line.
3. **Never fake a material.** Bevels, metal sheen, engraving and drop shadows belong to production (foil, emboss, casting), not to the mark. Rendering a primitive metal ornament literally failed in this project; flat marks succeeded.
4. **One flat ink must carry it.** Test in one colour, reversed, and on light, mid and dark grounds. Colour is a layer added on top, not the idea.
5. **Optical sizes.** Draw a separate small version for 16–48 px. What reads at 300 px can become a face at 24 px; at small sizes, use fewer, larger parts or none.
6. **Optical correction.** Round forms overshoot the baseline and cap height slightly. Make tapering strokes heavier at the joins. Keep gaps visually equal, not numerically equal. Make dots larger than the maths says when they sit near an edge.
7. **Misreading check.** For every option, name everything else it could look like (eye, face, ears, moustache, antennae, bean, a logo you know). Fix or drop it.
8. **Rhythm and rest.** Leave intervals (*ma*). Calm areas make busy areas sing. Tile any pattern 3×3 and look for clumps, holes and tramlines.
9. **Compare side by side.** Before and after, option against option, at the real size of use. Never judge a single option alone.
10. **Keep the failures.** Log what failed and why. Ideas outlive drawings: Bloom Within's idea lives on in the Ntina seed head.

## Reproducibility gate
Before calling a mark finished, it must pass all of these:
- one flat ink, positive and reversed
- 16 px and 24 px
- a stamp or stencil (no loose islands; see the studio's katagami check)
- an embroidery outline (no strokes under about 1 mm at the intended size)
- a screen print in one colour
- legible on the project's darkest and lightest grounds

## Learning from masters
Study one great designer or maker at a time. Take one concrete lesson and apply it to one piece, then show the before and after. So far: Ikko Tanaka (grid primitives, applied to the Ntina small size) and Charley Harper (the telling detail, applied to the D4 Tile).
