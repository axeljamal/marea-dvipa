# Dua Pulau — Origins & Recognition

*Dua pulau* means "two islands". This signature collection is our own original work, drawn from scratch. It grows out of two living traditions, and we name them every time we use it.

## Where it comes from

**Bomba, Central Sulawesi.** *Bomba* is the Kaili word for flower. The bomba flower is an old motif of the Donggala *buya bomba*, a hand-woven ikat sarong of the Kaili people, traditionally gold on black, worn at weddings, rites and in royal dress. Batik Bomba grew in Palu from 2008, when local makers learned batik with teachers from Pekalongan and began stamping bomba, clove, souraja and maleo designs. *Batik Bomba* is also a registered trademark of a Palu business (2015). Our collection is **Bomba-inspired** and not affiliated with any maker or brand.

**Bali.** The navy, teal and gold palette, the pearl keylines and the companion motifs come from the Balinese master edition (barong, candi bentar, lotus) built earlier in this project.

**Batik craft.** Isen-isen, the dotted and lined fills drawn with the canting, belong to the wider Indonesian batik tradition recognised by UNESCO in 2009.

**Mathematics.** Petal rings, ray counts and dot rings follow Fibonacci numbers and the golden angle. This is our own articulation and is not part of the source traditions.

## Motif by motif

| Motif | Inspired by | What we made our own | Meaning we honour |
|---|---|---|---|
| bomba-rose | Mawar bomba, the rose of the Donggala buya bomba sarong (Kaili people, Central Sulawesi), drawn from above with one stem and three leaves. | Petal rings in Fibonacci counts (13·8·5·3) turned by the golden angle; two-tone brushed petals with cecek veins; a golden-spiral heart; Bali teal and navy leaves. | In the buya bomba tradition the rose speaks of pure love for family, kingdom and God. |
| bomba-rosette | The same mawar bomba, as the open flower that gives Bomba its name ('bomba' is Kaili for flower). | Five Fibonacci petal rings (21·13·8·5·3): a pure phyllotaxis flower that tiles as a placement or a repeat. | Openness and togetherness, the meanings Palu people give the name Bomba. |
| rebung-border | Rebung (bamboo-shoot) borders of buya bomba, pointing inward, with the zigzag and dotted framing bands. | Two facing rows meeting at a gold zigzag spine with pearl cecek; sheaths drawn as nested chevrons; seamless horizontally. | In the cloth's iconography, inward-facing rebung stand for power, greatness and triumph. |
| ketupat-lattice | Belah ketupat (diamond) patterns in the head panel of buya bomba, known in Bugis as cobo'. | A seamless three-tone nested-diamond lattice with pearl cecek crosses at the joins. | Courage in proposing marriage and the wisdom of rulers and elders. |
| cengkeh-sprig | Cengkeh (clove) motifs of Palu batik, honouring Tolitoli's clove harvest. | A fan of five clove buds (a Fibonacci number) on curving stalks with one navy and one teal leaf. | Healing and wishes for the wearer's health and well-being. |
| dua-pulau-medallion | The meeting of two islands: Bomba's rose and rebung from Central Sulawesi, with the lotus-petal and keyline language of the Balinese master motifs. | Our emblem: a Bomba rose heart, 13 rebung rays, a ring of 55 cecek dots and 21 outer leaves. Every count is a Fibonacci number. | Recognition of both origins in one mark. |
| rebung-ribbon | The same inward-facing rebung borders of the Donggala buya bomba, whose framing bands run unbroken along the cloth. | A ribbon that is drawn live along any path, straight, diagonal, wave, golden spiral or golden arcs, with every shoot bending to the curve, rows offset by half a step, and a zigzag cecek spine. It skips curves too tight for the band rather than crumpling. | Power, greatness and triumph, now flowing with the pattern. |
| isen-cecek | Cecek, the dotted isen-isen fill of hand-drawn batik made with the canting, and the dotted framing bands of buya bomba. | A gently jittered staggered dot field that wraps seamlessly. | The patience of hand-drawn batik. |
| isen-zigzag | The zigzag lines that frame the central field of buya bomba. | Rows of zigzag with a periodic hand tremor, seamless in both directions. | The cloth's own framing language, used as texture. |
| isen-sero | Sero tangga, the Palu batik motif of the coastal fish trap, and sawut line fills. | Alternating blocks of fine parallel strokes, like woven trap panels. | Hard work that pays off; the lives of coastal fishing communities. |

## How to credit it

Wherever these motifs are published, printed or sold, include a line such as:

> Dua Pulau signature: original designs inspired by the Bomba flower of the Kaili people (Donggala and Palu, Central Sulawesi) and by Balinese ornament, with gratitude to the weavers and batik makers who keep these traditions alive.

## Care

- Ceremonial objects (taiganja pendant, sambulugana betel set) are deliberately left out of everyday prints.
- Meanings above are taken from the cited studies and local reporting. Some historical claims in those sources (for example dates for the Banawa kingdom) are not well verified, so we present them as tradition, not fact.
