02 · Why it matters

Four things decide whether a visitor books here or drifts back

A direct booking is the only one where the owner keeps the whole price, owns the guest relationship and can offer perks the platforms cannot.

Price and availability on the page

If the guest must leave your site to learn what a week costs, many never come back. Nightly rate, minimum stay and free dates inline remove the biggest reason to leave.

A reason to book direct

A visible strip of perks, a discount, late checkout, a welcome extra, gives the guest permission to skip the platform.

Speed and trust

Heavy page-builder sites load slowly on a phone on a train. A light page with good photography, real reviews and clear contact details reads as a well-run house.

Accessibility and the law

UK businesses have a duty under the Equality Act 2010 to make reasonable adjustments, and that includes websites. Alt text, keyboard navigation and labelled forms also help every guest, and search engines read the same signals.

05 · Our baseline

What every site we build includes

One HTML file, no framework, no page builder. It loads fast, is easy to host anywhere and needs no plugin updates.

Structure and performance

  • Single page with anchored sections and a mobile bottom tab bar; safe-area insets for notched phones.
  • Native scroll-snap carousels and a crossfade hero with a pause button, no carousel library.
  • Photos converted to JPEG at a maximum of 1800 px with location and camera metadata stripped.

Accessibility

  • Skip link, landmarks, labelled forms, visible focus, reduced motion respected.
  • Keyboard calendar grid with arrow, Page Up and Page Down navigation.
  • Light and dark theme from one set of colour tokens, following the system with a manual switch.
  • Alt text on every photograph; hero photos are real images, not backgrounds.

Booking

  • Live calendar with nightly rates, minimum stay, seasonal and midweek pricing and single gap nights priced inline.
  • Hold-dates dialog with validation, a currency switch and a three-perk strip under the quick-book bar.

Search and trust

  • JSON-LD: VacationRental or LodgingBusiness, Reviews, FAQPage, Organization, Place.
  • Real reviews, a map, directions and a self-serve guest guide.

Privacy

  • Non-modal consent card with Allow all, Essentials only and per-category toggles, reopenable from the footer.
  • Analytics and pixels load only after consent.
06 · Design principles

It should feel like the place, not like a template

Photography leads

One full-bleed hero rotating through the best photos, then a carousel beside each section. Put the thing guests come for first: for The Beam House that is the hot tub.

Restrained corners

Cards, frames and dialogs at 10 px; buttons, chips and inputs at 6 px. No pill buttons unless the brand already uses them. Circles only for true dots.

One palette, two themes

Every colour is a token with a light and a dark value, checked for contrast. Try the sun button in the bar.

Type that reads

A warm serif or humanist sans for headings, a plain sans for body, nothing below 16 px on a phone.

Motion that can be stopped

Slideshows and films have a pause control and respect the visitor's reduced-motion setting.

Nothing hidden behind a hover

Prices, perks and contact details are visible on first load, on a phone, without scrolling past a wall of marketing.

We offer each client three theme directions at the mock stage, as with Hearth, Sea Salt and Lantern for The Beam House, and build the one they pick.

    03 · Listing page anatomy

    Photos first, a rating beside the name, a price card that follows you

    All five platforms sell a property the same way. The differences are in who the badge comes from and how much of the house the guest is allowed to see.

    The platforms spend their trust budget on themselves: Superhost, Rare find, Home Critic. The property gets one photo mosaic, one paragraph and whatever the review count says. Nothing tells the guest about the brass bed, the owner, or why the house exists, which is exactly the ground an owner's own site can take.

    06 · The owner's edge

    What a listing never will do

    A platform page is a template shared with millions of other properties, ranked by an algorithm the owner cannot see, with the guest's contact details withheld.

    Cheaper for the guest at the same income

    On Airbnb's split fee a £1,000 stay costs the guest about £1,150. The direct site can charge £1,050, undercut the platform by £100 and still pay the owner £80 more. The perks strip should say this in plain numbers.

    The guest's email and the repeat booking

    Sykes keeps the address; Airbnb and Booking.com relay messages through their own systems. Only the direct booking gives the owner the relationship.

    The whole story

    Interactive tour, object stories, a film, the owner's voice. The platform allows one paragraph and a photo grid.

    Pricing rules the owner controls

    Midweek rates, gap nights, long-stay discounts and vouchers, set by the owner rather than by a Genius or Rare-find programme funded from the owner's margin.

    An assistant that knows the house

    The platform chatbots know the platform. Ours knows the hot tub temperature, the parking and the walk to the station, and can select the dates.

    Speed, accessibility and no ranking

    One file instead of 30 to 110 scripts, alt text on every photo, a skip link, dark mode. Found on the house's own name and never pushed down a list by a competitor paying for placement.

    The platforms remain the best place to be discovered. The owner's site is where the discovered guest should land, and where the second stay is booked.
